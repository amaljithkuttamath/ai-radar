# AI Radar — 2026-07-28

Frontier agentic models and open-protocol distillation headline this cycle, with Kimi K3’s debut marking a substantial technical leap—a massive mixture-of-experts architecture with vision and ultra-long context. ## Main list

### Agentic
**Agentic search techniques and open-sourcing of frontier capabilities are center stage.**
- [Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653) · [GitHub](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3) · [Project](https://www.kimi.com/blog/kimi-k3)  
Kimi K3 launches as a 2.8T parameter Mixture-of-Experts model—104B parameters active per inference—with a native 1M-token context window and vision. It leverages Delta Attention and Stable LatentMoE for deeper, longer-range information flow. Strong traction (212 HF upvotes) and usable artifacts. If you’re tracking scaling, attention innovations, or agentic workflows, this is a required read. · 4/5  
- [From Proprietary to Open-Source: Bridging the Distribution Gap via Multi-Agent Protocol Distillation in Agentic Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24280)  
Introduces a protocol for distilling multi-agent reasoning from proprietary models into open-source counterparts, using outcome-based RL supplemented by knowledge distillation for denser supervision. Noteworthy for addressing sparse reward issues in agentic search. Trending (61 HF upvotes), with strong-group authorship. Engineers and researchers focused on agentic tasks or knowledge-intensive retrieval will find novel, actionable guidance here. · 4/5

NVIDIA’s Cosmos3-Edge, tracked for a second run, remains stable in traction. Details on technical advances are spare, but the ongoing presence in trending models keeps it on the radar. · 3/5

## Insights
- Massive-context models are hitting practical openness, not just paper claims—Kimi K3 is public, with code and deployment details.
- Agentic search is converging: open distillation protocols now directly target proprietary model advantages, democratizing advanced capabilities.
- Production-grade releases (Microsoft, NVIDIA) are frequent, but sustained traction remains rare; utility trumps mere presence.
